# Product lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **The escrow flow is a real experience, not a feature list.** Deposit before work, 30% on draft approval, 70% on the live post, auto-release if the brand goes silent, and reclaim if the creator misses a deadline. Both sides can see that the money is real. That answers the most-cited pain in the brief: 48–87% of creators report payment problems.
- **The founder thought through the deal room from start to finish.** Brief, pitch, counter-offer, contract with FTC disclosure and usage rights, signature, promo code and results. Most of this runs in one place instead of across DMs, email and invoices.
- **The rate engine has something to say.** "You are underpriced" is a moment creators will feel, and about half of brands say they have mispriced deals.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **The trust core is not built, so the product's promise is not true yet.** Payments are a simulated ledger. Social stats are self-reported. Sales attribution is typed in by the brand. The pitch is "see their sales, data and socials," and today every one of those numbers is self-reported. The public "proven results" ROI rests on brand-reported conversions, and the performance bonus rests on creator-reported views. Each self-reported number is a seam where fraud or disputes get in. A marketplace whose numbers can't be trusted is worse than Instagram DMs, because it looks trustworthy.
2. **There is no one thing.** A brand marketplace, a creator storefront, a pricing engine, escrow, contracts, a results dashboard, three growth tiers with missions, a content log, a Hook lab and trend tracking all share one MVP. The growth tools are a second product for a second customer with a different job. Collabstr already offers listed packages, briefs and escrow with refunds. The parts that are the same as Collabstr can't be the reason to switch. The parts that differ are spread too thin to be excellent.
3. **The first five minutes for a brand is an empty room.** A brand that arrives with no verified creators in its niche or city, no reviews and no ROI history sees filters over nothing. Every ranking signal it is sold on (ROI, rating, proven results) only exists after deals have closed. The experience the pitch describes cannot happen on day one.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- In one sentence, with no "and": what does Collabo do that Collabstr does not?
- Walk me through a brand owner's first five minutes. What does she see, and why does she trust it?
- Which of these would you delete tomorrow: the Hook lab, trend tracking, missions or tiers? If none, why not?
- When a brand reports zero sales and the creator disputes it, who is right, and how does the product know?
- Real money moves in Stripe Connect, not in your app. Where does the experience hand off, and what breaks there (KYC, payout delays, refunds, Indian TDS/GST if you go there)?
- What is a typical deal size, and is the ~15% take worth it to that buyer? (Open question: /founder-consumer for willingness to pay, /founder-cfo for whether 15% covers payments and disputes.)
- Why would a buyer pick this over Collabstr's 10% fee or Instagram's reported 0% native affiliate tags? (Open question: /founder-competitors.)

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **Pick the one thing and say it in a sentence.** The strongest candidate is "creator deals where the money is guaranteed and the results are verified." Move the growth tools out of the brand-facing product, or freeze them.
2. **Make the trust core real before adding anything.** Live escrow on Stripe Connect, social stats pulled from platform APIs and not typed in, and attribution that does not depend on the brand's word (promo-code or store integration). Until then, label the ROI and stats as unverified.
3. **Design the cold start on purpose.** Choose one niche and one city (an open question in the brief), seed enough verified creators that a brand's first search returns real options, and show that first-search experience before scaling.
4. **Map every handoff and its failure mode:** sign-up, verification, deposit, draft upload (file uploads are not built yet), auto-release, disputes and payout, plus the notification for each (also not built). Escrow that auto-releases without notifications will cost a brand money without warning.
