# Monopoly lens
Score: 3 | Vote: PASS

## What is strong
- **A two-sided marketplace can build network effects**, and network effects are one of the few lasting advantages this lens recognises. If creators and brands in one niche end up on Collabo and nowhere else, that niche is hard to take from them.
- **A record of past results could become proprietary data.** Promo-code ROI per creator, built up deal by deal, is the one asset here a copycat cannot download. If it were verified, "proven results" would be something listings on other marketplaces lack.
- **The market is large and growing.** Spend is about $32.5B worldwide and about $12B in the US, growing around 16% a year. The pain points are real: payment problems, mispriced deals, flat mid-tier rates.
- **The MVP is broad and working**, so the open question is the market, not whether the product can be built.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **It is the same model as an existing player, at a higher price.** Collabstr already offers public creator packages, briefs, and escrow with a full refund, and charges brands 10%. Collabo takes about 15% in total (5% from the brand plus 10% from the creator). Insense, JoinBrands, InfluencerMetric (escrow, in India) and others sit within reach of the same buyer. Instagram's native affiliate tags reportedly take 0%. This is an incremental improvement in a crowded market, which is exactly the situation this lens warns against. Nothing in the brief is ten times better.
2. **No secret, and distribution is undefined.** Which side, niche or city to start in is still open, and so are the cost to acquire a brand and the cost to acquire a creator. A marketplace that is not dense in one small market has no network effect, only two empty sides. How the first 100 brands and 100 creators arrive, and what each costs, is unanswered.
3. **Users can go around the platform once they have met.** After one good deal, the brand and the creator can work together directly and skip the roughly 15% fee. The ROI data that could form a moat is self-reported and attributed by hand today, so it is not yet defensible, and a better-funded rival or the social platforms themselves could match it.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- What do you know about this market that Collabstr, Kofluence and TikTok do not? (the secret)
- Which small market (one niche, one city, one platform) could Collabo hold more than half of within 12 months, and why there? (/founder-competitors maps who already holds it)
- How do the first 100 brands arrive, and what does each cost? (open: brand and creator acquisition cost, /founder-cfo; willingness to buy, /founder-consumer)
- What average deal size and how many repeat purchases make a 15% take worth it, and why would a brand pay more than Collabstr's 10%? (open: deal size and frequency, /founder-consumer then /founder-cfo)
- What keeps the second deal between the same brand and creator on the platform?
- In ten years, what stops Instagram or TikTok from adding escrow and rate cards for free?
- Can you build verified stats and automatic attribution before a competitor does? Without them, the ROI record is not a moat.

## If FUND IF: the conditions
Not applicable; the vote is PASS. These would change it to FUND IF:
- A named beachhead (a niche or city) with evidence that Collabo can dominate it, and a competitor map from /founder-competitors that shows a gap nobody holds.
- A distribution plan with a costed path to the first 100 customers on each side (/founder-cfo).
- A lasting reason to stay on the platform after the first deal, such as verified ROI data that only exists on Collabo.
