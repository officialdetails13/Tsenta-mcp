# Offers lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **The market is real and growing.** US spend is about $12B and growing ~16% a year. The pain is documented on both sides: 48–87% of creators report payment problems, and about half of brands say they have mispriced deals. Escrow and the rate engine go straight at those two pains.
- **The stack has the right pieces.** Discovery, pricing, contract, escrow, staged release and results tracking each fix a real problem a brand hits on the way to "a creator post that sells". The 30/70 staged release and auto-release are thoughtful, and the deal room cuts real effort.
- **The dream outcome is the right one.** A small business buys sales, not posts. The promo code, ROAS dashboard and public creator ROI aim at that.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **Likelihood is close to zero today.** The value equation lives or dies on proof, and every proof point here is self-reported or manual: followers, engagement, views, and the sales that feed "proven results". A new marketplace has no deal history, so the ROI track record is empty when it matters most. A brand has no reason to believe this works better than scrolling Instagram.
2. **It is a pricier copy of an existing offer.** Collabstr already offers public packages, briefs, escrow and a full refund if the creator does not deliver, at a 10% brand fee. Collabo takes ~15% of deal value (5% from the brand, 10% from the creator) with less proof. Instagram's native affiliate tags reportedly take 0%. Without a clear "more outcome, faster, less effort", this competes on features, and the fee is not even lower.
3. **The buyer, and whether they have money, is undefined.** "Small businesses" is not a market you can reach. Nobody knows the deal size they will pay or how often they buy. Half the stack (missions, Hook lab, trend tracking) serves creators, who are not the side paying the most. If small-business deals are a few hundred dollars, a 15% take may not cover the cost of acquiring either side.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Which niche and city do brands have this pain in most, with budget already set aside? (/founder-consumer)
- What is the average deal size and how often do they buy? (/founder-consumer, /founder-cfo)
- What does it cost to acquire one brand and one creator, and how many deals before that pays back? (/founder-cfo)
- In one sentence, why would a brand pick this over Collabstr? (/founder-competitors)
- What is the guarantee on the outcome, beyond getting the post delivered?
- Could you charge the brand double and still be the obvious choice? If not, what is missing?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **One narrow market first.** Name a single niche and geography where brands have budget, and confirm the deal size and how often they buy in /founder-consumer before building more.
2. **Verified proof before launch.** Ship verified social stats (platform APIs) and automatic or code-verified attribution, so "proven results" are actually proven. Seed real case studies by hand-running the first deals.
3. **An outcome guarantee that Collabstr does not match.** For example a replacement creator or a partial refund if verified views or code sales miss an agreed floor, sized so /founder-cfo confirms it is affordable.
4. **Fees that work against the 10% benchmark.** Either beat it or justify the gap with the guarantee and proof, and /founder-cfo shows unit economics that work at the real deal size and acquisition cost.
5. **Cut the stack to what the brand pays for.** Creator growth tools stay only if they show they raise delivery quality or retention.
